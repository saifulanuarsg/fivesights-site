Our approach

From one place, you never see everything.

At Ryōan-ji in Kyoto, 15 stones sit in raked gravel. From any one spot on the viewing veranda you can see at most 14. Teams are the same: if everyone looks from one place, they miss something. So we ask five questions, in a set order, and keep your values in view the whole time.

I

Foresight

What mightbe coming?

Foresight is not prediction. Prediction says what will happen. Foresight gets you ready for what could happen.

More on Foresight

What conditions are already in place? What forces are already moving? What could come out of the changes we can already see?

Foresight is strongest when it comes out of Insight, Sight and Hindsight together. Without them, it is only guessing.

Methods we use here

Scenario planning (Shell, Peter Schwartz) · Three Horizons (Sharpe, International Futures Forum) · Futures wheel · STEEP and PESTLE trend scanning · Backcasting from a target future · Causal Layered Analysis (Inayatullah) · Weak signals and wildcards scanning

  1. 1/3 · Questions it asks

    What changes are already underway?

  2. 2/3 · Questions it asks

    What could knock us off the path we are on?

  3. 3/3 · Questions it asks

    What are we assuming is stable that might not be?

The values question

Which futures would we refuse to build, even if they paid?

II

Insight

What mightothersmiss?

Insight is about meaning: what the facts tell us.

More on Insight

Where do the patterns point? What do they tell us about how things really work?

Insight without Hindsight is guesswork. Insight without Sight is theory with no facts behind it.

Methods we use here

Jobs to be done (Christensen) · Systems thinking and leverage points (Donella Meadows); the iceberg model · First-principles reasoning · Theory of Constraints (Goldratt) · Mental models (Senge); the ladder of inference (Argyris) · Ethnographic and design research

  1. 1/3 · Questions it asks

    What behaviour are we observing, and what would explain it?

  2. 2/3 · Questions it asks

    What constraint is actually active here?

  3. 3/3 · Questions it asks

    What would someone need to believe for this to make sense?

The values question

Whose explanation are we taking as the true one, and why theirs?

III

Sight

What isactuallyhappening?

Sight is about now: what is in front of you, without the story you tell about it.

More on Sight

It is the hardest of the five. You have to describe what you see, not what you expected, wanted or feared to see.

The other four sights depend on it, as the other pieces of the swan rest on the neck. Hindsight without it becomes nostalgia. Foresight without it becomes fantasy.

Methods we use here

Gemba walks (Toyota, Lean) · OODA loop, the Observe step · Endsley's three levels of situational awareness · Sensemaking (Karl Weick) · Cynefin probe, sense, respond (Snowden) · Operational dashboards and leading-indicator KPIs

  1. 1/3 · Questions it asks

    What are we observing, and what are we assuming?

  2. 2/3 · Questions it asks

    What are people doing, not what do they say they are doing?

  3. 3/3 · Questions it asks

    Where is the gap between the stated process and the lived one?

The values question

What are we choosing not to look at?

IV

Hindsight

What hasalreadyhappened?

Hindsight is learning from the past. It is not nostalgia, and it is not blame.

More on Hindsight

It means looking carefully at your own past, your industry's past and the wider world's.

Hindsight teaches Foresight. You cannot anticipate what you have not learned to recognise. Many teams confuse it with Insight: Hindsight is what happened and the patterns in it, Insight is what those patterns mean.

Methods we use here

After Action Reviews (US Army) · Agile retrospectives and blameless post-mortems · Five whys and Ishikawa cause analysis · Double-loop learning (Argyris and Schön) · Lessons-learned registers and decision journals

  1. 1/3 · Questions it asks

    Where have we seen this condition before?

  2. 2/3 · Questions it asks

    What did we do last time, and what happened as a result?

  3. 3/3 · Questions it asks

    What early signals did we miss, and how would we see them now?

The values question

What did we give up last time the choice got hard?

V

Oversight

What are weresponsiblefor?

Oversight is about responsibility: who you answer to, and what you must protect.

More on Oversight

What are you responsible for? What needs protecting?

Most teams skip it because it feels like compliance, not strategy. If you skip it, you can make plans that hurt people or that rely on things you cannot keep up. Oversight makes you ask who pays the cost of your decision.

Methods we use here

Three Lines model (Institute of Internal Auditors) · RACI and DACI accountability mapping · Stakeholder salience model (Mitchell, Agle and Wood) · Balanced Scorecard, used as a governance lens · COSO ERM, fiduciary duty mapping and ESG materiality

  1. 1/3 · Questions it asks

    Who depends on this system working?

  2. 2/3 · Questions it asks

    Where are we responsible but not in control?

  3. 3/3 · Questions it asks

    What costs are we leaving to someone else?

The values question

Who pays for this decision, and did they have a say?

Values

Beneathall five,as the stone

Your values show in what you spend money on, and what you protect when a choice is hard.

What you give up under pressure shows them too.

Two teams can ask the same five questions well and reach different conclusions, because their values differ. Every method carries values, ours too. We put your values on the table, so you talk about them openly instead of letting them steer the decision unseen.

"We are not making you into something you're not. We are shaping your values."

Methods we use here

Schwartz theory of basic human values · Barrett Cultural Values Assessment · Stated-versus-revealed values audit · Spiral Dynamics, as a values-development lens

Where a method belongs

Each method sits under the question it helps answer. A few, like Cynefin and the Balanced Scorecard, fit under two.

Where the mark comes from

A bird cut into five pieces.

"We are not making you into something you're not. We are shaping your values."

Drawn by Ibnur Rashad after the 2026 Asia Pacific Futures Network gathering in Bangkok, as a foresight mark grounded in Singapore.

  1. A black swan.

    A rare, high-impact event that looks predictable only afterwards, as Nassim Nicholas Taleb describes it in The Black Swan (2007). We do not claim to predict one. We train teams to be ready when one lands.

  2. Cut into pieces.

    Each piece is one sight. Look from one place and you see one piece. The full view needs all of them.

  3. The red point is Foresight.

    It points forward, and it is the red dot of Singapore, where the practice and its method were made.

  4. The pieces fold into a stone.

    The total area does not change. The stone is your organisation's values. Every sight is cut from it.
